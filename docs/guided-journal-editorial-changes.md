# Guided journal writing and editorial preview changes

The six guided entry types retain their stages, storage keys, labels and validation rules. There are no new minimum word counts. Labels remain familiar; the new guidance asks for context, evidence and reasoning. Creator-only optional markers remain in the editor; reader previews omit empty responses and question labels.

## Creator field guidance

| Existing label (retained) | New guidance shown beneath the input |
|---|---|
| Discovery Title | Give this entry a short title that captures what you noticed or want to understand. |
| What was observed? | Describe what you noticed, including a specific example, change, or experience. What details would help someone who was not there understand it? |
| Location | Where did this happen? Include the place or setting if it helps explain the situation. |
| What is already known? | What do you already know from experience, earlier work, or other sources? Separate what is known from what you still need to check. |
| Root Cause | Name one possible underlying cause. Treat it as an explanation to investigate, rather than a confirmed conclusion. |
| Short Description | Describe how this cause could contribute to what you observed. |
| Why do you think this occurs? | What observation, experience, or source supports this explanation? Note any assumptions or other possible explanations. |
| Why was this pattern selected? | Explain how this kind of change could address the situation. What would be different, and who or what could benefit? |
| Challenge Title | Give the challenge a short, recognizable name. |
| Challenge | Describe the situation you want to improve, why it matters, and the people, places, or systems involved. |
| Description | Describe the idea clearly enough for someone else to picture it. What would change? |
| How would it work? | Explain the steps or mechanism behind the idea. Why might it address the challenge? |
| Strengths | What makes this idea useful or practical in this context? Consider its benefits, resources, and accessibility. |
| Challenges | What could make this idea difficult to carry out? Note constraints, tradeoffs, or questions to investigate. |
| Name | Give this part of the project a name others will recognize. |
| Purpose of the Component | What role does this part play in the wider project, activity, or system? |
| Key Features | List the characteristics that matter for its purpose, use, or performance. |
| Build Title | Name the work or change documented in this entry. |
| Objective | What are you trying to create, change, learn, or evaluate? Explain what a useful outcome would look like. |
| Build Process | Describe what you made, assembled, or changed and the order you worked in. Explain important choices and adjustments so someone else could follow your approach. |
| Design Notes | Record decisions, constraints, unexpected changes, or details worth remembering for the next iteration. |
| Test Title | Give this test or investigation a short, specific name. |
| Method | Explain what you did, what you observed or measured, and the conditions you kept consistent. Include enough detail to repeat or compare the test. |
| Expected Outcome | Before considering the results, describe what you expected to happen and what would indicate success or useful progress. |
| Feedback & Notes | What actually happened? Include specific observations or results, explain the rating you chose, and note limitations or feedback. |
| Technical Analysis Title | Name the question, comparison, or calculation explored here. |
| Baseline Description | Describe the starting conditions or reference case. Include relevant quantities, units, timeframe, and assumptions. |
| Proposed Description | Describe the alternative conditions or approach. Use comparable boundaries, units, and timeframes where possible. |
| Field Notes | Explain your calculations or interpretation, including units, sources, assumptions, and uncertainty. What can the evidence support, and what still needs checking? |
| Implementation Title | Name the activity, change, or solution put into practice. |
| Summary | Describe what was put into practice, how it was carried out, and who or what was involved. Include relevant details and evidence of what happened. |
| What worked well? | Describe a specific success and what helped it happen. What would you keep or repeat? |
| What didn't work well? | Describe what fell short, changed unexpectedly, or remained difficult. What might explain it? |
| Key Takeaways | What have you learned from putting this into practice? Explain how the experience changed or strengthened your understanding. |
| What would you change? | What would you adapt next time, and why? Connect the change to what you observed or learned. |
| What did you learn today? | Explain one insight from this entry and the observation, evidence, or experience behind it. How has your understanding changed? |
| What challenges or questions came up? | What remains uncertain, difficult, or unresolved? Explain why it matters and what could help you investigate it. |
| What are next steps? | Describe a specific next action, why it follows from this entry, and what you will look for to judge progress. |
| Open Notes | Add anything else worth keeping: an unexpected observation, a connection, a question, or a note for a collaborator. |

### Existing question labels using the same new guidance

- **What Challenge are we trying to solve?** uses the Challenge guidance above.
- **Describe the Idea** uses the Description guidance above.
- **What are the strengths of this idea?** uses the Strengths guidance above.
- **What are the challenges of this idea?** uses the Challenges guidance above.
- **Title** asks for a short recognizable title.

Labels remain unchanged to preserve familiar inputs; the new questions and follow-ups are guidance beneath those labels. Existing required/optional status and character limits remain unchanged.

## Interactive elements

| Element | Guidance / behavior |
|---|---|
| Objectives | “What changes would count as progress? Add 1–4 specific objectives you can use to compare your ideas.” Existing count limits retained. |
| Ideas | “Explore up to four approaches. Explain how each could work, then use evidence and shared criteria to compare them.” |
| Decision matrix | Criteria remain tied to objectives; weights total 100%, each idea is rated 1–5 using the same standard. |
| Question types | How: process or improvement; Where: location of pattern/need/effect; What: what is happening/matters/could change; When: timing and change over time. |
| Inquiry lens | Select the approach used in the entry and reflect on how it helped make sense of the work. Taxonomy unchanged. |
| DCI / SEP / CCCs | Select concepts and practices actually used and connect them to experience or evidence. |
| People / affected communities | Existing selection and permissions remain unchanged; chosen identities remain metadata rather than narrative. |
| Materials and currency | Existing item names, nonnegative costs, currency choice and no-materials option retained. The reader sees recorded items and totals without editing actions. |
| Data sources | Existing source categories retained. Source and calculation icons stay visible; uncertainty is prompted in Field Notes. |
| Outcomes and ratings | Existing outcome options retained. Feedback & Notes asks for observations, explanation of rating and limitations. |
| Evidence captions / descriptions | “Describe what this evidence shows, how it relates to your notes, and any limits or uncertainty.” |
| Images / sketch / graph / table / video / link | Existing creation, upload and validation controls retained. Their icons and saved captions remain in the preview. All six are shown in the evidence review page. |
| Timeline | Existing create-new-timeline action retained. No linkage selector is added to previews. |

## Shared instruction wording: previous → revised

| Previous wording | Revised wording |
|---|---|
| Set up the context for this ideation session. | Describe the situation you want to improve and what useful progress would look like. |
| What do we hope to achieve with these designs or ideas? Add at least 1 objective, up to 4. | What changes would count as progress? Add 1–4 specific objectives you can use to compare your ideas. |
| Add up to four ideas to compare. | Explore up to four approaches. Explain how each could work, then use evidence and shared criteria to compare them. |
| Choose the lens that best describes your thinking for this entry. | Choose the lens that best describes how you approached this work. Use it to reflect on how you made sense of the experience. |
| Select NGSS components demonstrated in this entry. | Choose the science ideas, practices, and connections you actually used. Select those you can connect to this entry’s experience or evidence. |
| Describe what this evidence shows and why it matters. | Describe what this evidence shows, how it relates to your notes, and any limits or uncertainty. |

## Reader presentation

- Continuous unboxed sections, readable paragraphs, adaptive paragraph spacing, no invented transitions.
- The desktop review hides question labels and narrative labels by default. Show prompts restores their context. Metadata labels remain visible.
- Learning selections are grouped in one expandable disclosure and retain existing icons.
- Blank fields and empty direct-field panels are omitted. Assessment remains absent from previews.
- Existing evidence renderers are retained in the Flutter app. The separate desktop review supports image enlargement, saved sketch geometry, graph plus data table, tables, playable video, and links.
