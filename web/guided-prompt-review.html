Guided journal writing and editorial preview changes

The six guided entry types retain their stages, storage keys, labels and validation rules. There are no new minimum word counts. Labels remain familiar; the new guidance asks for context, evidence and reasoning. Creator-only optional markers remain in the editor; reader previews omit empty responses and question labels.

Creator field guidance

Existing label (retained)New guidance shown beneath the input
Discovery TitleGive this entry a short title that captures what you noticed or want to understand.
What was observed?Describe what you noticed, including a specific example, change, or experience. What details would help someone who was not there understand it?
LocationWhere did this happen? Include the place or setting if it helps explain the situation.
What is already known?What do you already know from experience, earlier work, or other sources? Separate what is known from what you still need to check.
Root CauseName one possible underlying cause. Treat it as an explanation to investigate, rather than a confirmed conclusion.
Short DescriptionDescribe how this cause could contribute to what you observed.
Why do you think this occurs?What observation, experience, or source supports this explanation? Note any assumptions or other possible explanations.
Why was this pattern selected?Explain how this kind of change could address the situation. What would be different, and who or what could benefit?
Challenge TitleGive the challenge a short, recognizable name.
ChallengeDescribe the situation you want to improve, why it matters, and the people, places, or systems involved.
DescriptionDescribe the idea clearly enough for someone else to picture it. What would change?
How would it work?Explain the steps or mechanism behind the idea. Why might it address the challenge?
StrengthsWhat makes this idea useful or practical in this context? Consider its benefits, resources, and accessibility.
ChallengesWhat could make this idea difficult to carry out? Note constraints, tradeoffs, or questions to investigate.
NameGive this part of the project a name others will recognize.
Purpose of the ComponentWhat role does this part play in the wider project, activity, or system?
Key FeaturesList the characteristics that matter for its purpose, use, or performance.
Build TitleName the work or change documented in this entry.
ObjectiveWhat are you trying to create, change, learn, or evaluate? Explain what a useful outcome would look like.
Build ProcessDescribe what you made, assembled, or changed and the order you worked in. Explain important choices and adjustments so someone else could follow your approach.
Design NotesRecord decisions, constraints, unexpected changes, or details worth remembering for the next iteration.
Test TitleGive this test or investigation a short, specific name.
MethodExplain what you did, what you observed or measured, and the conditions you kept consistent. Include enough detail to repeat or compare the test.
Expected OutcomeBefore considering the results, describe what you expected to happen and what would indicate success or useful progress.
Feedback & NotesWhat actually happened? Include specific observations or results, explain the rating you chose, and note limitations or feedback.
Technical Analysis TitleName the question, comparison, or calculation explored here.
Baseline DescriptionDescribe the starting conditions or reference case. Include relevant quantities, units, timeframe, and assumptions.
Proposed DescriptionDescribe the alternative conditions or approach. Use comparable boundaries, units, and timeframes where possible.
Field NotesExplain your calculations or interpretation, including units, sources, assumptions, and uncertainty. What can the evidence support, and what still needs checking?
Implementation TitleName the activity, change, or solution put into practice.
SummaryDescribe what was put into practice, how it was carried out, and who or what was involved. Include relevant details and evidence of what happened.
What worked well?Describe a specific success and what helped it happen. What would you keep or repeat?
What didn't work well?Describe what fell short, changed unexpectedly, or remained difficult. What might explain it?
Key TakeawaysWhat have you learned from putting this into practice? Explain how the experience changed or strengthened your understanding.
What would you change?What would you adapt next time, and why? Connect the change to what you observed or learned.
What did you learn today?Explain one insight from this entry and the observation, evidence, or experience behind it. How has your understanding changed?
What challenges or questions came up?What remains uncertain, difficult, or unresolved? Explain why it matters and what could help you investigate it.
What are next steps?Describe a specific next action, why it follows from this entry, and what you will look for to judge progress.
Open NotesAdd anything else worth keeping: an unexpected observation, a connection, a question, or a note for a collaborator.

Labels remain unchanged to preserve familiar inputs; the new questions and follow-ups are guidance beneath those labels. Existing required/optional status and character limits remain unchanged.

Reader presentation

Continuous unboxed sections, readable paragraphs, adaptive paragraph spacing, no invented transitions.

The desktop review hides question labels and narrative labels by default. Show prompts restores their context. Metadata labels remain visible.

Learning selections are grouped in one expandable disclosure and retain existing icons.

Blank fields and empty direct-field panels are omitted. Assessment remains absent from previews.

Existing evidence renderers are retained in the Flutter app. The separate desktop review supports image enlargement, saved sketch geometry, graph plus data table, tables, playable video, and links.
